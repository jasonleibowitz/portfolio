## The problem

There is always more to watch than anyone could get through. The problem is choosing, and on your own that is twenty minutes of scrolling. With other people it is twenty minutes of scrolling and a negotiation. The apps meant to help share three flaws, and Reel is built around all three.

1. **Rating in the abstract does not work.** Is Forrest Gump a 4.5 or a 5? I will not give the same answer twice. Ask whether I liked it more than Finding Nemo and the answer is immediate, and it is still true a year later.
2. **A new app knows nothing about what you have already seen.** There is no realistic way to enter a thousand films you have watched, so it spends its first months recommending things you saw years ago.
3. **A recommendation you cannot act on is not one.** Being told to watch something excellent on a service you do not subscribe to just moves the problem along.

## What it does

Reel asks you to compare, not to score. You place a film by answering a few quick this-or-that questions against films you have placed already. It takes seconds, and it produces a ranking you still agree with a year later.

Nobody starts from nothing, so you can also log films you have already seen without ranking each one, which turns a back catalog of hundreds into a single sitting. What comes out is your library: what you have watched, what you mean to watch, your own notes, and a film page carrying Rotten Tomatoes, IMDb, TMDB and Metacritic alongside your score.

From there Reel learns what you actually like, and every recommendation tells you where the film is streaming, so it reads "watch this, it is on your Netflix" rather than sending you to a service you do not pay for.

Reel is a single-player app first, because a recommendation engine that does not know you is worth nothing to your friends either. Following people, matching taste between libraries, and choosing something for a room rather than a person are what comes after.

## How it's built

A Django API and an Expo app in one monorepo, with the mobile client's types generated from the API's own schema, so the two halves cannot drift apart without the build saying so. Film data comes from TMDB and OMDB, kept current by background jobs and insulated well enough that a slow provider costs a detail rather than the whole screen.

How a pile of comparisons turns into a score, and into a recommendation, is the part I would rather not publish.

## What I'd do differently

I built the ranking mechanic first and assumed the rest would follow from it. It works, and it was never where people fell off: everyone who signed up ranked films and finished the flow. What I had not built was a reason to open the app on a night you did not watch anything.

The fix was not a better algorithm, it was recommendations and knowing where things stream. I would have found that sooner by measuring the app before building the next thing for it.
