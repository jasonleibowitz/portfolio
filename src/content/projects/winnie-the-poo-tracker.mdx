## The problem

During the height of the Covid lockdown I was diagnosed with Crohn's Disease. My doctor wanted me to keep a log of all of my _bowel movements_ to report back to him. As someone who likes spreadsheets and apps I looked for an existing app that would do this for me, but they were all pretty _shitty_. So I built Winnie the Poo Tracker as a very simple _BM_ log that surfaces interesting and important stats for both you and your doctor.

## How it works

The app is pretty simple. The main screen is just a log of your _BMs_, then stats surfaces important and interesting stats and trends for you to share with your doctor.

When building a health-focused app, privacy is of paramount concern. I also wanted to focus on a fully featured offline-first app. The app uses TanStack Query (with `persistQueryClient`) and MMKV. All data is optimistically updated locally. If the user is offline data syncs as soon as they're back online.

There is no separate API and all logic is processed client-side in the app.

## What I'd do differently

This was my first React Native app and I was focused on making it an entirely offline experience. For simplicity and speed I used Supabase for authentication and the database, but wanted the app to work fully offline. Initially I set up PowerSync as a Database Sync Engine. Unfortunately their free tier killed the app after a short period of inactivity and their paid tier wasn't cheap.

I rebuilt the app with TanStack Query handling any offline persistence and syncing.
