So You Want to Get an Espresso Machine?

21 May 2023 ·12 min read ·Jason Leibowitz

If you're wondering whether you should get an espresso machine, just don't. Making quality espresso at home is so much harder than drip coffee — you'll have to deep-dive into coffee nerd-dom to get anywhere near what your local third-wave shop serves.

But if you're ready to embark on this journey, it can be incredibly fun and rewarding. Here's everything I wish someone had told me before I spent the money.

Types of espresso machines

Four categories are worth knowing about, and the differences matter far more than the badge on the front. Price tracks control, not quality.

Super automatic

Grinds, brews, froths and hands you a finished cup at the press of a button. Incredibly easy to use; the coffee is mediocre, to put it mildly.

Semi automatic

You control the grind, the dose, the tamp and the shot time; the machine controls the water. This is where nearly everyone should start.

  • The grinder matters more than the machine.
  • PID temperature control is worth it; pressure gauges are not.
  • A bottomless portafilter tells you the truth about your technique.
“Buy the grinder first. Everyone who ignores this buys the grinder second, at twice the price.”

Manual lever

No pump, no electronics, total control over the pressure profile. The best shots I've pulled at home, and the most demanding every single time.

Mobile strategy

  • Nav: fixed bottom tab bar, four items at flex: 1 with icon + 11px label, 62px tall plus env(safe-area-inset-bottom). Thumb-reachable and always visible. The top strip keeps only the wordmark and theme toggle.
  • Cost: it permanently occupies ~62px of vertical space and reads as "app" rather than "blog" — body gets matching bottom padding so nothing hides behind it.
  • Cards are a single column below 640px, two columns above; the feature card spans both.
  • The ticker strip is overflow: hidden — it can never widen the document — and its animation is disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Post hero is a full-bleed colour band; the cover image pulls up into it with a negative margin that is safe at every width.
  • Tap targets. Every control and chrome link — nav, tags, buttons, cards, the wordmark — is ≥44px tall, measured at 360px; tab bar items are 62px. Inline links inside a sentence are text links: they clear the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum of 24×24 but are deliberately not padded to 44px, which is the standard inline exception.

Dark mode

  • Wire it up. html[data-theme] + blocking inline script; the toggle sits in the top strip on every breakpoint.
  • Colours stay saturated in dark; only their lightness moves. Cream becomes near-black with a blue cast rather than neutral grey.
  • Delete html.dark from global.css; replace with @custom-variant dark bound to the attribute.
