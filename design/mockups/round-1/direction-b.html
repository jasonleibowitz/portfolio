So You Want to Get an Espresso Machine?

2023-05-21 12 min 2,410 words
Contents
  1. Just don't do it
  2. Types of espresso machines
  3. What to actually buy
  4. The grinder problem

If you're wondering whether you should get an espresso machine, just don't. Making quality espresso at home is far more difficult than drip coffee, and you will have to deep-dive into coffee nerd-dom to get anywhere near what your local third-wave shop serves.

Types of espresso machines

Four categories are worth knowing about. The differences between them matter far more than the badge on the front — price tracks control, not quality.

Super automatic

Grinds, brews, froths and presents a finished cup at the press of a button. Incredibly easy; the coffee is mediocre.

Semi automatic

You control the grind, dose, tamp and shot time; the machine controls the water. This is where nearly everyone should start.

  • The grinder matters more than the machine.
  • PID temperature control is worth it; pressure gauges are not.
  • A bottomless portafilter tells you the truth about your technique.

Buy the grinder first. Everyone who ignores this buys the grinder second, at twice the price.

Manual lever

No pump, no electronics, complete control over the pressure profile. The best shots I've pulled at home, and the most demanding every single time.

Direction B

Index — design spec

One family, two weights, a strict 8px grid and exactly one colour. Hairlines instead of cards. Dates and durations set in monospace so lists align down the page. The design gets out of the way; the content inventory becomes the interface.

Typeface

Role Family Package
Everything Inter Variable (400 / 500 / 600) @fontsource-variable/inter
Dates, counts, captions JetBrains Mono (400) @fontsource/jetbrains-mono

Two files total. Mockup substitutes the system sans + system mono.

Type scale — tight, mostly fixed

Token Size Use
t-xs 12px mono labels, dates, captions, footer
t-sm 13px row descriptions, code
t-md 14px nav, tables, secondary UI
t-base 16px index rows, UI body
t-lead 18px article body, intro paragraph
t-h2 20 → 24px clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.5rem)
t-h1 32 → 44px clamp(2rem, 1.5rem + 2.2vw, 2.75rem)

Only h1 and h2 scale with viewport — everything else is fixed, which is what keeps the grid strict. Tracking −0.028em on headings, −0.006em on body. Measure 36rem.

Near-black rather than pure #000, so hairlines stay visible. Body contrast 18.9:1 light, 16.1:1 dark. faint is used only for non-essential metadata (4.0:1) — it is paired with, never replacing, text that carries meaning.

Spacing — strict 8px grid

8 — u1
16 — u2
24 — u3
32 — u4
48 — u6
64 — u8
96 — u12

No half-steps. Gutter clamp(16px, 8px + 2.2vw, 32px), section clamp(48px, 24px + 5vw, 96px). Shell 60rem.

Mobile strategy

  • Nav: native <details> disclosure. A 44px-tall Menu button opens a full-width panel of 48px rows. Keyboard and screen-reader behaviour comes free; no JS, no focus trap to get wrong. Swaps to an inline row at ≥704px.
  • The index rows are a one-column stack below 704px (date line above title) and a three-column grid above it. Same markup, no duplication.
  • Header is sticky and only 56px tall on mobile.
  • Article figures are full-measure with a 1px border rather than full-bleed — nothing needs to escape the container.
  • Tap targets. Every control and chrome link — nav, footer, tags, chips, buttons, the wordmark — is ≥44px tall, measured at 360px. Inline links inside a sentence and post-title links inside a heading are text links: they clear the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum of 24×24 but are deliberately not padded to 44px, which is the standard inline exception.

Dark mode

  • Wire it up. html[data-theme], blocking inline script in <head>, localStorage over prefers-color-scheme.
  • Toggle is a persistent ☾ button in the header — in a near-monochrome design the theme switch is the one piece of chrome worth surfacing.
  • Delete html.dark from global.css; add @custom-variant dark (&:where([data-theme=dark] *)).
