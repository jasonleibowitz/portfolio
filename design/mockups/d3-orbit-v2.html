So You Want to Get an Espresso Machine?

coffeereview
21 May 202312 min read2,410 words
Contents
  1. Just don't do it
  2. Types of espresso machines
  3. The grinder problem
  4. What to actually buy

If you're wondering whether you should get an espresso machine, just don't. Making quality espresso at home is far more difficult than drip coffee, and you'll have to deep-dive into coffee nerd-dom to get anywhere near what your local third-wave shop serves.

But if you're ready to embark on this journey, it can be incredibly fun and rewarding. Here is everything I wish someone had told me before I spent the money — starting with the part nobody wants to hear, which is that the machine is not the expensive bit.

Types of espresso machines

Four categories are worth knowing about, and the differences matter far more than the badge on the front. Price tracks control, not quality — a $2,000 super-automatic will lose to a $400 lever in the right hands, and the gap only widens as you learn what you are doing.

Semi automatic

You control the grind, dose, tamp and shot time; the machine controls the water. Start here. A 58mm bottomless portafilter will tell you the truth about your technique.

The grinder problem

Buy the grinder first. Here's the ratio I use to work out whether a shot was under- or over-extracted: 

// grams in → grams out, over time
export function brewRatio(dose, out, seconds) {
  const ratio = out / dose;
  if (ratio < 1.5) return 'ristretto';
  if (ratio > 2.5) return 'lungo';
  return seconds < 22 ? 'under' : 'dialled in';
}

Everyone who ignores this buys the grinder second, at twice the price. It is the single most repeated mistake in home espresso, and the one that costs the most to undo.

Orbit v2 — what changed

Your notes, applied

  • Section spacing. One --section token, clamp(44px, 24px + 2.6vw, 72px), used by every block; adjacent sections no longer double up their padding, and heading→content is a fixed --head-gap of 24px. v1's gaps were roughly 40% larger and drifted per block.
  • The cycling word. ship → last → delight → scale, 2.8s each, cross-fading with a blur and a 4px rise; the container animates its width so the full stop doesn't jump. Frozen on the first word under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Theme toggle. The icon morphs sun→moon, and the change itself sweeps out as a circle from the button using the View Transitions API. Browsers without it just swap instantly — no polyfill, no layout shift.
  • Monogram. A JL mark replaces the anonymous dot: gradient squircle, the J's bowl and the L's stem sharing a baseline. Works as a favicon at 32px and as an avatar wherever you need one.
  • Experience. Three placeholder bullets per role, a pulse travelling down the spine, and a toggle so you can compare even spacing against duration-proportional spacing.
  • Writing index. One list, no year headings. Date, read time and tags on every row, plus a sticky tag rail with counts that filters in place. On mobile the rail becomes a scrollable chip row above the list.
  • Post. Measure 40rem → 46rem and body type up to 18–22px. A reading-progress bar sits under the header in the same gradient as the hero word, on both desktop and mobile.
  • Footnotes. Clicking a reference opens a popover in place instead of throwing you to the bottom; the ↩ in the footnote list scrolls back to the reference and opens that popover so you don't lose your place.
  • Post tags. Promoted from small print at the bottom to chips under the title.
  • About. A photo grid sized for three or more images.
  • Dark mode. Lifted off near-black: base #0B0B12 → #16161F, text #ECEAF5 → #F4F2FB, and the violet and cyan both lightened so they read against it.

Projects: frontmatter contract

Projects become their own collection, and the homepage renders only the ones flagged is_featured — so adding a project never silently changes the front page. 

// src/content.config.ts
const projects = defineCollection({
  loader: glob({ pattern: '**/*.{md,mdx}', base: './src/content/projects' }),
  schema: z.object({
    title: z.string(),
    description: z.string(),
    status: z.enum(['building', 'beta', 'live']),
    stack: z.array(z.string()),
    is_featured: z.boolean().default(false),  // ← homepage
    order: z.number().optional(),
    links: z.object({ site: z.string().optional(), repo: z.string().optional() }).optional(),
    draft: z.boolean().default(false),
  }),
});
  • Homepage: (await getPublished('projects')).filter((p) => p.data.is_featured) — through getPublished, per the repo's rule that no route calls getCollection directly.
  • Today that means Winnie the Poo Tracker and Reel Watch are featured; leibowitz.me is not — it lives on /projects only, since a site that links to itself from its own front page is a bit much.
  • order is there for when there are enough projects that "newest first" stops being the right sort.

Judgment calls — tell me if you disagree

  • Projects stayed on the homepage, but as two large cards with device shots rather than the small ones. For a portfolio the apps are the strongest asset, and a visitor who never leaves the homepage should still see them. Cutting the section is a two-line change if you'd rather.
  • Writing stayed too, with the blurb you suggested, three latest posts, and an inline link in the hero paragraph as well. It's the lightest section on the page.
  • Photos are a grid, not a carousel — everything visible at once, no JS, nothing hidden from search. A carousel makes sense above about eight photos.
  • Duration-proportional timeline is built but off by default. It reads honestly, though a short role gets visually squeezed and a long one leaves a hole — try the toggle and see.
