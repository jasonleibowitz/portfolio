Recommendation: D2 Console. You're sending this to engineering hiring managers, and it's the one that signals "this person builds things" in the first two seconds without saying it. It also handles your content best: the repo-style cards suit two apps with real stacks, the deploy-log experience section reads as a career rather than a list, and the line-numbered code panel makes the footnotes-and-code requirement look deliberate instead of bolted on. D3 is the one to pick if you'd rather be remembered than understood — it's the most striking, and the aurora plus glass is genuinely distinctive, but it's also the most likely to feel dated in two years and the heaviest to keep consistent. D1 is the safe floor: nothing in it will ever look wrong, and nothing in it will ever be the reason someone remembers the site.

What changed since round 1

  • The homepage is a résumé now. Hero → at a glance → experience → projects → writing. The blog is the last section on the page rather than the whole page.
  • Projects is a real section — an index plus a case study per app, with a sticky spec rail on desktop listing status, platform, stack and role. Winnie leads (nearer release), Reel Watch second, both marked TestFlight.
  • The headshot swap survives and now works on touch: hover on desktop, tap on mobile, chip stays lit until you tap another. It's about 15 lines of vanilla JS in the layout — no React island, so the site still ships zero islands.
  • The ticker is gone. So is the cream background — all three sit on #FCFAF6, the one you preferred.
  • Kept from B: the at-a-glance stats block and the year-grouped index with date, read time and tags on every row. The article measure went from 36rem to 40rem, and the table of contents is in all three.
  • Footnotes and code are designed, not assumed — the post screen shows both. Production side is Astro's built-in Shiki plus remark-gfm and remark-footnotes.
  • About stayed, as you asked: long bio, résumé PDF, and a "beyond the CV" row of fun stats. Nav is Home / Projects / Writing / About, with Lists as a tab inside Writing — that keeps the bottom bar at four slots, which is the most that fits cleanly at 320px.

What's still placeholder — everything marked with a dotted underline in the mockups, so you can see the gaps rather than trust a summary:

  • Employment dates and job titles. The repo only told me the order of the companies, so I did not invent them.
  • One-line pitches for Winnie the Poo Tracker and Reel Watch, plus all case-study body copy.
  • App screenshots — the phone frames are drawn in CSS. Real screenshots drop straight in.
  • The About bio and the "beyond the CV" numbers. Once you pick a direction I'll draft the bio with you — countries visited is a good one; cities lived in, concerts, a "currently reading" slot and a small map are other options.

Honest weaknesses

  • D1 — the least memorable of the three. It is a very well-made version of a layout a lot of good engineers already have. If "modern and techy" was the brief, this is the one that answers "modern" and mostly skips "techy".
  • D2 — the monospace and the ~/ paths are a deliberate bet. Engineers read it as fluency; a non-technical recruiter may read it as noise, and lowercase headings can look like an affectation if you're not into it. Easy to dial back: keep the panels and the amber, set headings in sentence case.
  • D3 — backdrop-filter on a dozen elements per page is the expensive part, and it's the direction most likely to trip the performance audit (#10). Cyan is only 3.1:1 on the light base, which is why it never carries body text — that constrains the palette more than it looks. It is also the most "of its moment".

How this was verified

Every screen of every direction was measured in Chrome rather than eyeballed — document.documentElement.scrollWidth against viewport width at 320, 360, 390, 414, 768 and 1280px in both themes, plus a tap-target sweep at 360px.
